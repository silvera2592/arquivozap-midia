Renting a home in England, or helping a son or daughter into their first flat? Two things come up before the keys do: the right to rent check and the deposit. Right to rent (England only) • The landlord or agent checks every adult aged 18 or over who'll live there • British or Irish citizens can show a passport, current or expired. No passport? Two documents together, such as a UK birth certificate and a driving licence • Not British or Irish? You can usually get a share code online on gov.uk The money • A holding deposit, paid to reserve the place, can be up to 1 week's rent • The tenancy deposit is usually capped at 5 weeks' rent • Rent in advance is usually limited to 1 month, and can't be taken before the agreement is signed The deposit On most private tenancies it has to be protected within 30 days in one of three schemes: Deposit Protection Service, MyDeposits or Tenancy Deposit Scheme. You should be told in writing which one, and each scheme can check for you. It's due back within 10 days of you and the landlord agreeing the amount. If you can't agree, the scheme's dispute service is free. If it was never protected, a court can order the landlord to pay you up to 3 times the deposit. Keep from day one: the tenancy agreement and written terms, the deposit scheme details, receipts, the gas safety record if the home has gas, and the inventory. The rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, so check locally.
